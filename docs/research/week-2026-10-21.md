[F73](facts.md#f73) checks the candidate that adds array and hash mutation sites as writers to F72's rule.
It admits the witness values of 52 of F72's 56 losing rows. Still, 21 rows lose real values, 17 of them new:
13 through routing or missing contributions, 6 in container contents and 2 others. Its equality gains are not
sound gains, and the sampled census bounds the loss rate only loosely.

[F74](facts.md#f74) names the cause of Mastodon's remaining schedule variance: a reader of absence inside a cycle.
A class guard turned a not-yet-computed argument into a Hash, and a six-slot cycle kept that premature value in
some schedules only. With Pending kept pending at the guard, all ten Mastodon tables agree: on F73's candidate
across four schedules, and on a port to upstream main across twelve. The change costs precision in 24 rows and
leaves F73's losses and twelve structural identities open.

[F75](facts.md#f75) builds the controller-binding table once, after completion, from completed method bodies.
On top of F74's guard, all four apps reach 10/10 equal tables, and Forem and Chatwoot no longer vary. The
independent check corrected the binding audit: 482 rows change in meaning, 472 bindings are added and 104 known
values lose precision, 95 of them in Chatwoot. Both the old and the new table miss 45 bindings that Ruby reaches
through calls inside hash arguments or string interpolation. That gap predates the change and stays open.

[F76](facts.md#f76) makes the send catalog wait while an operand is entirely Pending. The 21 catalog strictness
failures pass, all four apps keep 10/10 equal tables, and no value set changes. It costs 3.6–7.7% more Pending at
the completion barrier, and one Chatwoot reason row's provenance now depends on the schedule. The other laws
still have 916 FAIL and 123 UNKNOWN rows, and older catalog gaps such as `Int + Float` typed `Int` remain.
